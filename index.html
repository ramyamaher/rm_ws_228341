About

I am an architect with projects in several countries. I studied architectural engineering and hold the LEED Accredited Professional credential. I have lived in three countries and speak five languages.

My interest in technology, programming, finance and other cultures led me to create several apps and to start an archive of places.

Revitesse

Architecture and engineering software is built to serve 90 to 95 percent of the market. The remaining 5 to 10 percent is where the work becomes highly specific, and that is where most of the time is lost.

Working in different offices, I met the same problems again and again. Revitesse grew out of the wish to solve them for good, and to help other professionals spend less time on repetitive tasks in Revit.

The archive

I started it after a long time away from my home country. I realized that I had become a citizen of the world: a nomad in time as much as in space, who belonged to each place only at a particular moment. Nostalgia, for me, is conditional, because places change faster than anyone can grasp.

James Joyce is said to have asked his aunt to visit a house in Dublin and check whether an overweight man could plausibly climb over its railings. If the city were ever burned to the ground, he wanted it to be possible to rebuild it from Ulysses. That story is where my archive began.

Architecture

Wohnpark Gebersdorf

Nuremberg, Germany Invited competition, 2016 First prize

A new residential quarter on the site of a former electrical converter station between Gebersdorfer Straße and Bibertstraße in Nuremberg. Besides housing, the brief called for a daycare centre, senior apartments and a neighbourhood grocery store.

The winning scheme lines up terraced houses along a green promenade that crosses the site, with play lawns, a playground and an oval daycare pavilion along its length. Four-storey apartment buildings face Gebersdorfer Straße and shelter the quarter from traffic, while the senior housing, the grocery store and a café frame a small square. A forest path follows the western edge, and a wildflower meadow doubles as rainwater storage.

Museum of the 20th Century, berlin modern

Berlin, Germany Herzog & de Meuron Competition won in 2016, under construction

The new museum for the Nationalgalerie's collection of twentieth-century art completes Berlin's Kulturforum. It stands on the last open site of the ensemble, between Mies van der Rohe's Neue Nationalgalerie, Hans Scharoun's Philharmonie and the Church of St. Matthew.

Under a single wide pitched roof, with façades of open brickwork, the building is conceived as a covered piece of the city. Two passages cross inside it and connect it to the streets and squares on every side, so that art is met on the building's edges as much as in its galleries. Beside the Nationalgalerie's holdings it will show the Marx, Pietzsch and Marzona collections, and a tunnel links it underground to the Neue Nationalgalerie.

Design

Layers of Egypt: Between Labyrinth and Elysium

Egypt Pavilion, La Biennale di Venezia 15th International Architecture Exhibition, 2016 Second place

A competition entry for Egypt's national pavilion at the 2016 Venice Architecture Biennale, awarded second place. The pavilion is a passage between two worlds. Visitors first find their way through a dark labyrinth whose walls are covered with photographs of Egypt's cities, quarries and crowds, and with texts on its ancient sites, while a short poem traces the way from frustration towards a distant light.

The labyrinth opens into a bright hall under a luminous ceiling, where a large model of Greater Cairo and the Giza plateau lies on a single table. Virtual-reality headsets hang from above, and visitors move between the model and immersive views of the city.

Extended Threads

Master's project 2016

Extended Threads joins a philosophical reading of architecture with technology. It takes apart what architecture means, who builds, where and how, and puts it back together as a game.

In a gesture-based video game played in virtual reality, players use their hands to control robots at a distance. The robots build by sintering, fusing loose material layer by layer into solid form, across a lunar landscape. The game runs on a computer and on a phone in a VR headset, and many players can build together from anywhere.

Revitesse

Revit, but faster.

Revitesse is a plugin for Autodesk Revit that takes the repetitive work out of producing drawings. It began as a paid product and is now completely free and open source, installed as a pyRevit extension.

Its tools sit in five panels on the Revit ribbon: Views, Sheets, Parameters, Revision Clouds and Annotations.

  • Create many sheets at once and place views on them in one step
  • Create shared parameters in batches, and transfer or combine their values
  • Select and filter elements, or the tags on them, by category or parameter
  • Number every door and window in the project at once
  • Number, override, list and export revision clouds
  • Find, select and replace text in annotations
Open source on GitHub Tutorials on YouTube

Bancanca

Personal finance in a single file.

Bancanca keeps a household ledger, shows where the money goes and projects where it could go. The whole app is one HTML file: it runs offline, needs no server and no account, and the data never leaves the device. An Android app wraps the same file.

  • A ledger for several people, accounts and currencies
  • Bank statement import and reconciliation
  • Spending analysis, budgets and recurring payments
  • Investment and property projections, adjusted for inflation
  • Summary and tax reports
  • PIN lock and dark mode

Shuin 朱印

A world travel stamp.

A shuin is the vermilion seal a Japanese temple gives its visitors. Shuin, the app, turns every trip into a map: routes are drawn across the world, each country visited earns a stamp in a passport of 200 designs, and the whole journey can be played back as an animation. It runs in the browser and on Android.

  • An interactive world map of routes and cities
  • A passport with a stamp for every country
  • Journey playback along a timeline
  • Statistics, expenses and the CO2 of every leg
  • Shareable images of the map
  • Friends and shared trips
